A Windows desktop app for paced personalized email on the user’s own Outlook account.

The problem

Outreach at any real volume from a personal Outlook account has two failure modes: being flagged as spam for sending too fast, or losing track of who was contacted, who bounced and who already opted out, especially across campaigns that run for weeks.

How I solved it

  1. Built the core workflow: prospect import, template creation, message drafting, paced sending and bounce cleanup, with one rule: nothing sends without a person clicking Send.
  2. Paced sending with randomized 60–90 second gaps instead of a bulk-email service.
  3. Gated every send on three live checks: a daily cap, a real connectivity test and a working-hours window.
  4. Persisted each prospect’s state (pending, drafted, sent, bounced, skipped, failed) in a local SQLite database.
  5. Added hygiene tools: a spam-wording preview per prospect, an inbox bounce scanner that proposes removals, and an opt-out and cooldown check before any draft.

The outcome

  • Paced outreach with zero paid infrastructure; pacing controls do not guarantee inbox delivery or prevent spam classification.
  • No accidental over-sending: a resumed campaign never fires a burst of email at 2 a.m.
  • Crash-safe campaigns: an interrupted run resumes exactly where it stopped and never re-sends.
  • No duplicate or unwanted contact across campaigns, and bounce cleanup reduced to a short review step.
