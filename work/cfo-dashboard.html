The problem

Executives reviewing a sales report can easily mistake volume for value. A segment with large sales may contribute very little profit once discounts and costs are counted.

The goal was a single executive view that compares sales, profit, margin and units across time, customer segments, countries and products.

How I solved it

  1. Cleaned the dataset: 700 rows from 2013–2014 with currency-formatted fields converted for calculation; no missing values in the supplied file.
  2. Defined the executive KPIs: revenue, profit, margin percentage and units sold.
  3. Built the report in Power BI: a revenue and profit trend line, profit by country, and revenue by segment, with KPI cards for the headline numbers.
  4. Compared segment scale with profit contribution to find where margin is thin.

The outcome

  • Net sales total about 118.73M across 2013–2014, with 9.21M in discounts against 127.93M gross.
  • Government is the largest segment at 52.50M in sales and 11.39M in profit.
  • Enterprise is the warning sign: 19.61M in sales but only about 162,776 in profit, a much thinner margin than the other segments.
  • Recommendation: revisit pricing, discount policy and portfolio mix for the weaker segments.
